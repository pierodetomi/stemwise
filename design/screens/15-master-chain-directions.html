15 · Master chain, three ways to show it

The exploration that led to the rack (A-080, A-081); kept as a record. With repeatable modules the chain is a list that grows: up to twelve blocks, two of a kind at most. The same master in three layouts, each with a visible way to add a module. The author chose R.

G

Grid

Cards in rows with an “Add module” tile after the last free module. Familiar and wordy: every card has room for a value and a detail. The editor loses a row of height, and the order reads in two directions (along a row, then down).

R

Rack (chosen)

The chain is a column beside the editor, top to bottom, as the inserts of a channel. One row per module with its value, its reduction and its switch; “Add module” is a row of its own where the new block would land. Twelve rows fit without scrolling and the order reads in one direction. The editor is narrower and taller.

P

Path

One column per module, left to right, with a bar that says how far the peaks stand above the body after it: the chain shows at a glance which module does the work and what is left to the limiter. A “+” sits between every two modules and an “Add module” column before the limiter. Names and values are tight at twelve modules.
